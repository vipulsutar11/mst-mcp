# Web2 and Web3

**Web2** is the internet as we know it today — platforms and apps like social media, e-commerce, and online services. Most data is stored on central servers owned by companies. Users interact through web browsers and apps, but control of the platform stays with the provider.

**Web3** is the next phase of the internet, powered by blockchain technology. It focuses on decentralisation, giving users ownership of their data, digital assets, and identities. Instead of relying on a single company, it uses peer-to-peer networks and smart contracts.

---

## Difference between Web2.0 & Web3.0

| Feature | Web2 | Web3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Control** | Controlled by companies | Controlled by users and communities |
| **Data Storage** | Centralised Servers | Decentralised on blockchain |
| **Payments** | Traditional banks, payment gateways | Cryptocurrencies, smart contracts |
| **Security** | Company-managed | Blockchain-based, tamper-resistant |
| **Identity** | Email/password accounts | Blockchain wallets & keys |

---

## Centralisation vs Decentralisation

Web2.0 relies on **centralised systems** operating on a hub-and-spoke model — one central authority manages and controls everything. Web3.0 relies on **decentralised systems** operating like a web — no single point of control, and decisions are made collectively by the network.

| Centralised Systems | Decentralised Systems |
| --- | --- |
| Information travels quickly because a single authority handles it with powerful resources. | Information may take longer to spread, as it passes through multiple participants. |
| Higher performance and easier to build. | Lower performance and more complex to set up. |
| Conflicts are easy to resolve — the central authority decides. | Requires special protocols to resolve disputes between participants. |
| **Single point of failure** — if the central authority is attacked, the whole network can go down. | **No single point of failure** — the network keeps working even if many participants are attacked. |
| Easy to coordinate updates and changes — the central authority can enforce them. | Harder to coordinate updates — disagreements can split the network. |
| The central authority can censor or block participants. | Censorship is difficult because data can travel many paths. |
| Participation is controlled by the central authority (gatekeeping). | Anyone can join — no gatekeepers, low barriers to entry. |

> *Note: These points describe general trends, not absolute rules. Most networks exist on a spectrum depending on design, purpose, and governance.*

---

## Advantages of Web3

* **User Ownership:** You control your data and assets.
* **Transparency:** All transactions are visible on the blockchain.
* **Security:** Tamper-proof and resistant to hacks.
* **No Middlemen:** Direct interactions between users.
* **Global Access:** Anyone with internet can participate.

---

## Limitations of Web3

* Slower transaction speeds compared to Web2.
* High network fees in some cases.
* Requires technical knowledge to use.
* Limited scalability for mass adoption.

---

## Overcoming Web3 Limitations with Web2 + Web3

By combining Web2 and Web3, applications can keep the speed, scalability, and ease of Web2 while adding the ownership, transparency, and security of Web3. This hybrid model makes blockchain-powered applications faster, cheaper, and easier for everyone to use.
